# APrompter — Privacy policy

_Last updated: 26 October 2026_

APrompter is a teleprompter app made by **night owly**, an individual developer ("the
developer"), who is responsible for this policy and for the data described in it. Privacy
questions and requests: **nightowlyyyy@gmail.com**.

Short version: **APrompter never sends your scripts or videos anywhere. There is no account,
no sign-in, no ads and no tracking, and the app contains no analytics or crash-reporting tools;
the only figures the developer gets from it are purchase figures in RevenueCat. The only network
traffic is for APrompter Unlimited: the app asks RevenueCat whether you have it and which plans
are on sale (when the app starts, when it comes back on screen, and on the upgrade screen), and
sends the store's receipts when you buy or restore, or when the store reports a purchase or
subscription change the app hasn't passed on yet.** Your phone's own backups and photo sync work
outside the app — see "Things outside APrompter's control".

## APrompter Unlimited (purchases)

Free use is limited to one script (long scripts show a reminder). Removing that limit is a
subscription or a one-time purchase made through the Apple App Store or Google Play. The app uses
RevenueCat (RevenueCat, Inc., USA), a payment-infrastructure provider, to check what you've
bought.

- **What's sent:** a random identifier made for this install of the app (never your name,
  email or phone number); on iPhone also Apple's "identifier for vendor", an ID iOS gives one
  developer's apps on your phone, which resets when you delete them; your purchase and
  subscription status and the receipts from Apple or Google; and basic technical data needed to
  deliver them: app version, phone model, Android or iOS version, language settings,
  country/storefront and IP address, and — when RevenueCat turns on its diagnostics — timings
  and error codes of these purchase checks. On Android, Google Play also receives a scrambled
  (hashed) form of the random identifier with each purchase.
- **When:** when the app starts and when it comes back on screen (your status and the plans are
  re-checked if the last check is more than a few minutes old; purchases or subscription changes
  the store reports, for example after a reinstall or a renewal, are sent straight away), when
  you open the upgrade screen, and when you buy or restore.
- **What it's used for:** to unlock, restore and keep track of APrompter Unlimited, to catch
  fake receipts, and to show the developer purchase figures (purchases, renewals,
  cancellations) in RevenueCat. Never for ads or tracking, and no decisions about you are made
  automatically.
- **What's not sent:** your scripts, notes, videos, settings, or anything else in the table
  above apart from the purchase row. There is no sign-in, so the developer doesn't know who you
  are.
- **RevenueCat's role:** RevenueCat handles this data only to provide its service to the
  developer, under its data processing terms, and must protect it at least as well as this
  policy describes. Apart from RevenueCat, and Apple or Google as your app store, no company
  gets data from the app. RevenueCat's own policy: https://www.revenuecat.com/privacy/
- **Where it's processed:** RevenueCat processes this data in the United States. Transfers
  from the EU, the UK and other places with rules on sending data abroad rely on the safeguards
  in RevenueCat's data processing terms, such as the European Commission's standard
  contractual clauses.
- **How long it's kept:** RevenueCat keeps the identifier, purchase history and technical data
  until APrompter is withdrawn from the stores (a lifetime purchase can be restored years
  later), or until you ask for them to be deleted (see "Your rights").
- **Payment details** (card, billing address) are handled entirely by Apple or Google; the app
  and RevenueCat never see them.
- **Restoring:** a purchase belongs to your App Store or Google Play account. *Restore
  purchases* asks the store for your purchases and sends the receipts to RevenueCat, which
  links them to this install's random identifier. You don't sign in to APrompter for this.
  On Android, once there is a purchase (bought or restored), RevenueCat also saves its random
  identifier in Google's Block Store, which goes into your Google account backup if backup is on (end-to-end encrypted when
  your phone has a screen lock). After a reinstall, or on a new phone set up from that backup,
  RevenueCat links the new install to it so your purchase records are found again.
- **No selling or sharing:** the developer doesn't sell or share personal information (as
  California law uses those words). APrompter doesn't track you across apps or websites, so it
  works the same whatever your "Do Not Track" setting.

## Importing and sharing

- **Into APrompter:** when you share a note or file to APrompter (or paste, or pick a file),
  it is read and converted on your phone. On iPhone, the share extension passes it to the app
  through a private folder on the phone that only APrompter can read. It waits there until you
  next open APrompter, which takes it in and deletes it from that folder. Cancelled shares are
  removed straight away, and deleting the app removes the folder. Nothing is uploaded.
- **Out of APrompter:** *Share* and *Save as text file* hand your script to the app or place you
  pick (Notes, Keep, Files, Drive, email…). What happens to it there is up to that app.

## Permissions and why

- **Camera and microphone** — on while the Record screen is open, for the preview. Video and
  sound are recorded only when you tap *Record*. Nothing is streamed or uploaded.
- **Photos / gallery** — to save your takes into an "APrompter" album. On Android 10 and
  older, Android asks for access to photos, media and files for this; newer Android versions
  don't ask. On iPhone, the app asks for photo access so it can keep takes in its own album; if
  you allow access to only some photos, iOS may not let it use the album, and takes are then
  saved to your library without it. The app only adds its own takes. It doesn't read, change or
  upload your other photos or files.
- **Display over other apps** (Android) — only for *Float*, the prompter window you put on top
  of other camera apps. The window is marked secure, so screen recordings and live streams
  show it blank.
- **Notifications** (Android) — if you allow them, Android shows a notification while the
  floating window is running.
- **Face ID / fingerprint** — only if you turn on *App lock*. The check happens on your
  phone; APrompter never sees your biometric data.
- **Internet** — only for the APrompter Unlimited checks described above.

You can turn camera, microphone, photos, *Display over other apps* and notifications off at
any time in your phone's settings (iPhone: *Settings → APrompter*; Android: *Settings → Apps →
APrompter → Permissions*, with *Display over other apps* under *Special app access*). Only the
matching feature stops working. Face ID / fingerprint is used only while *App lock* is on, which
you turn off in APrompter's settings. Internet can't be switched off per app on Android; on
iPhone you can turn off *Cellular Data* for APrompter.

## Security

- Everything sent to RevenueCat travels encrypted (HTTPS).
- Your scripts, settings and the takes you keep in the app stay in the app's private storage,
  which other apps can't read. *App lock* also asks for Face ID, fingerprint or your screen
  lock before the app opens.
- In RevenueCat, APrompter's purchase records can be seen only by the developer, and by
  RevenueCat to run its service.

## Things outside APrompter's control

- **Device backups.** Depending on your phone settings, your phone's own backup to your Google
  or Apple account may include APrompter's data: your scripts, settings and the purchase
  identifier. Takes kept inside the app are left out of iCloud and Google backups, but a direct
  phone-to-phone transfer from a phone on Android 12 or later brings them along.
- **Gallery sync.** Takes saved to the gallery are synced if Google Photos / iCloud Photos sync
  is on. Keep takes inside the app to avoid this.
- **Clipboard.** The app reads the clipboard only when you tap *Paste*. *Copy as caption* puts
  text on it, and your keyboard or Apple's Universal Clipboard may pass it to your other
  devices.
- **Crash reports and store statistics.** If you let your phone share diagnostics with app
  developers, Apple or Google may send the developer reports when APrompter crashes (phone
  model, system version, app version and where it crashed), and they show the developer totals
  such as downloads per country. This comes from Apple or Google, not from APrompter, and never
  includes your scripts or videos.

## Children

APrompter is meant for people aged 13 and older and isn't directed at children under 13. It
doesn't ask anyone, including children, for names, emails or other personal details. The only
data APrompter itself sends off the phone is the purchase information described under
"APrompter Unlimited", used as described there and never to identify, track or advertise to
anyone. If you're under 18, ask a parent or guardian before buying APrompter Unlimited. If you
believe a child under 13 has bought it, email nightowlyyyy@gmail.com and the records will be
deleted.

## Why this data is used

- **Checking, restoring and delivering APrompter Unlimited:** needed to give you what you
  bought. For people who haven't bought, checking at launch whether a purchase exists is the
  developer's legitimate interest in running the app; without it Unlimited can't be bought or
  recognised, and the rest of the app works the same.
- **Catching fake receipts, purchase figures and the purchase checks' timings and error
  codes:** the developer's legitimate interest in keeping purchases working.
- **Emails you send:** to answer you.

## Your rights

**On your phone:** delete scripts in the app (and empty *Recently deleted*), or uninstall the
app to remove what it stored on the phone, with one exception on Android: once there is a
purchase and Google backup is on, the purchase identifier RevenueCat keeps in Google's Block
Store stays with Google Play services on the phone and in your Google backup after you
uninstall, so a reinstall picks it up again. Copies in your phone's backup stay until you delete
them in your Google or Apple account's backup settings. Videos in your gallery are deleted in
the gallery app.

**Purchase records:** you can ask for a copy of them, or for them to be corrected, deleted or no
longer used, wherever you live. Email nightowlyyyy@gmail.com with the subject "My APrompter
data", the store you bought in and the order number from the store's receipt email (Google
Play: it starts with "GPA."; App Store: the Order ID). The app never shows its identifier, so
the order number is how your records are found; if you never bought anything, the developer has
no way to find them. To have them deleted, uninstall APrompter first. Requests are answered
within 30 days. Deleting the records doesn't cancel a subscription, so cancel it in your App
Store or Google Play account first. Your purchase stays with your store account and can still
be restored later, which starts a new record.

You can also complain to the data protection authority where you live.
